What to Look For

Memory Foam Density and Thickness

Look for memory foam beds with at least 2-3 inches of high-density foam (4-5 lb density is ideal). Thicker foam provides better joint support and lasts longer without bottoming out. Cheap beds with thin, low-density foam compress within months and lose all therapeutic benefits for arthritic dogs.

Orthopedic Support Features

The best orthopedic beds include raised edges or bolsters that support the neck and back, plus contoured designs that distribute weight evenly. Look for waterproof memory foam cores and removable, machine-washable covers—essential for senior dogs prone to accidents or incontinence.

Size and Breed Appropriateness

Your senior dog needs a bed where they can fully stretch out without hanging off the edges. Measure your dog's length and width when lying down, then add 6-8 inches. Senior dogs especially benefit from extra space since arthritis makes curling up painful.

Non-Slip Bottom and Durability

Hardwood and tile floors are slippery for elderly dogs with weak back legs. Choose beds with rubberized, non-slip bottoms that prevent sliding. Also verify the cover material can withstand multiple washes and that seams are reinforced—senior dog beds get heavy use.

Why Protein Matters More for Active Dogs

Working, sporting, and high-drive dogs can need 25–30% more protein than the average couch companion. Protein supplies the amino acids that rebuild muscle fibers after hard exercise, support tendon and ligament health, and keep coats glossy through seasonal shedding. A quality chew shouldn't just entertain — it should contribute meaningfully to that daily protein budget. Look for chews where a named animal protein (beef, chicken, salmon, lamb, bison) is the first and ideally the only significant ingredient, with minimal added starch or sugar. Air-dried and dehydrated formats tend to preserve more nutrition than heavily processed, extruded chews.

How to Choose (and Serve) the Right Chew

Match the chew to the dog, not the marketing. Power chewers need denser formats like tendon or yak cheese; light chewers and puppies do better with jerky strips and lung bites. Always size up — a chew should be longer than your dog's muzzle so it can't be swallowed whole — and supervise until the piece is small enough to discard. Keep chews to roughly 10% of daily calories so you don't unbalance a carefully planned diet, and store air-dried products in a sealed container away from heat. Finally, introduce any new protein slowly over a few days, especially with sensitive-stomach dogs. If you're building a starter kit, a mixed pack of Gnarly Pet jerky plus one long-lasting chew covers about 90% of real-world use cases.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much protein does an active dog actually need?

Most active, working, or sporting dogs do well on a diet containing roughly 25–30% protein on a dry-matter basis, compared to around 18–22% for less active adults. Protein-packed chews and jerky can supplement that intake, but treats should stay under about 10% of total daily calories. Check with your vet before making major dietary changes, especially for dogs with kidney or liver conditions.

Are natural dog chews safe for aggressive chewers?

Many are, as long as you choose the right density and size. Beef tendon, gullet, and yak cheese hold up well to power chewers, while soft jerky is better suited to training and light chewing. Always pick a chew longer than your dog's muzzle, supervise the session, and take away any piece small enough to swallow whole.

Can I use Gnarly Pet chews as training treats?

Absolutely — that's one of their biggest strengths. Gnarly Pet jerky tears cleanly into pea-sized pieces, so a single strip can produce a dozen or more rewards. That makes it ideal for high-repetition work like recall, loose-leash walking, and place training without overfeeding your dog.

How should I store natural, preservative-free dog chews?

Keep them in an airtight container in a cool, dry place away from direct sunlight. Because natural chews skip artificial preservatives, they can dry out or lose aroma if left open. For soft jerky, resealing the bag after each use — or refrigerating in humid climates — keeps them fresh and appealing to your dog.

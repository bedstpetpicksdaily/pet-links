What to Look For

1. Interior dimensions — not exterior

This is where most buyers get burned. Measure your current litter box (including a hood or high sides) and compare it to the interior width, depth, and height listed in the specs. Leave at least 2–3 inches of clearance on each side so your cat can turn around comfortably. Large-breed owners with Maine Coons or Ragdolls should target interiors of at least 24" x 20".

2. Ventilation and odor management

A sealed box traps ammonia and can discourage cats from using it at all. Look for enclosures with rear cutouts, slatted panels, side vents, or a gap at the top. Bonus points for models that include a spot to mount a carbon filter or a small deodorizer.

3. Cleanable, moisture-resistant materials

Raw MDF and untreated particleboard swell and stain fast. Prioritize laminated or melamine-coated engineered wood, sealed solid wood, or rattan panels with a wipeable interior. A removable or waterproof floor panel makes weekly deep cleans a two-minute job instead of a chore you avoid.

4. Real access for humans

You'll be scooping daily, so check how the unit opens. Front doors with magnetic catches, hinged tops, and full-height double doors all beat a tiny side hatch. Also confirm the entrance hole is wide enough (roughly 8"+) and, ideally, positioned so litter falls back inside rather than onto your floor.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will my cat actually use a litter box inside furniture?

Most do, but transition matters. Place the enclosure in the box's existing spot, leave the door open for a few days, and skip any drastic litter changes during the switch. Cats that already use a covered box adapt almost immediately; cats used to an open pan may need a week. If your cat is skittish, choose a model with two openings so it never feels cornered.

Does an enclosure make the smell worse?

Only if it's poorly ventilated or you scoop less often. A vented enclosure with daily scooping actually contains odor better than an open box because it limits airflow of dust and smell into the room. Add a carbon filter pad inside the top panel and use a clumping, low-dust litter for best results.

Are these hard to assemble?

Expect flat-pack assembly in the 30–60 minute range with an included hex key — comparable to a basic bookshelf. Reviews from the last few months are the best assembly-quality signal; look specifically for mentions of pre-drilled hole alignment, since that's the most common complaint on cheaper units.

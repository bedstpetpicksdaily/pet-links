Why This Matters

Pet food recalls happen for serious reasons—typically contamination, mislabeling, or the presence of harmful substances that could endanger your cat or dog. While manufacturers and retailers work to remove affected products from shelves, some items may still be in homes. Staying informed protects your pet's health and helps you make confident purchasing decisions going forward.

Beyond this specific recall, it's a reminder to stay vigilant about pet product safety and to know where to find reliable information quickly when concerns arise.

What Pet Owners Should Know

  • Check the FDA website and the specific brand's recall notice for affected batch numbers, lot codes, and expiration dates
  • If you have a recalled product, stop feeding it to your pet immediately and contact your veterinarian if your pet has shown any signs of illness
  • Keep receipts and product packaging for returns or refunds
  • Consider brands with transparent sourcing and quality testing practices
  • Follow reputable pet safety accounts and subscribe to FDA recall alerts

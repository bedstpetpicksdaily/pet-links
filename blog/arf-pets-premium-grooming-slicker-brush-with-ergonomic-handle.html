Frequently Asked Questions

What coat types does this brush work best for?

The Arf Pets handles double coats (Labs, Retrievers, Corgis, Huskies) excellently because the wire density is tight enough to reach undercoat without being punishing. Single-coat dogs (Poodles, Maltese) will work fine but you're slightly overpaying for features you don't need—a wider-spaced slicker would be sufficient. Curly and wavy coats need monitoring; the brush can grab rather than glide, so use shorter strokes and don't force it through mats.

How does Arf Pets compare to FURminator and other popular brands?

FURminator is technically a deshedding tool, not a slicker brush—different job. If you're comparing Arf Pets to Chris Christensen or similar premium slickers, you're looking at slightly thinner wires on Arf Pets, which means marginally more control required but significantly lower cost. Arf Pets will last 18-24 months with daily use; premium brands stretch to 3+ years. For most home groomers, Arf Pets wins on value. For professionals grooming 8+ dogs daily, the premium brands justify their cost through durability and speed.

Is the handle really that much better than cheap alternatives?

Yes, meaningfully so. Cheap slicker handles are typically thin plastic that concentrates all pressure into your palm's center, causing fatigue. Arf Pets' handle is wider and contoured, which spreads the work across more of your hand and prevents the pinching sensation. If you're grooming for 10 minutes or less weekly, you won't notice. If you're doing 30+ minutes per session, you'll feel the difference—your hand stays fresher and your strokes stay consistent longer.

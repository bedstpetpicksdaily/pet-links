Why Freeze-Dried Raw Is the Smartest Fuel for Active Dogs

Freeze-drying removes moisture at low temperatures under vacuum rather than blasting food with high heat. That matters for performance dogs because heat is what degrades amino acid availability, natural enzymes, and heat-sensitive vitamins. A freeze-dried raw patty or slider retains the protein integrity of the original muscle meat, organ, and bone — which is precisely what a dog needs to rebuild after a long hike, a flyball tournament, or a weekend of chasing a frisbee across a chilly field. Higher digestibility also means more of the meal is actually absorbed rather than passing through, which translates to smaller stools, steadier energy, and less GI distress on the road.

How to Feed Freeze-Dried Raw for Fall Performance

Start by calculating roughly 2–3% of your dog's ideal body weight in raw-equivalent food per day, then bump that by 10–25% on high-output days — long hikes, hunting trips, agility trials, or sub-freezing weather where thermoregulation burns extra calories. Rehydrate freeze-dried sliders with warm water or Smallbatch bone broth for about five minutes before serving; this improves hydration and slows fast eaters. If you're transitioning from kibble, mix in about 25% raw for the first several days and increase gradually over one to two weeks. For travel, pre-portion sliders into zip bags — a week of meals for a 50-pound dog weighs a fraction of what canned or frozen food would.

What to Look For in Any Freeze-Dried Dog Food

Not all freeze-dried raw is created equal. Look for named whole-muscle proteins listed first, included organ meat (liver, kidney, heart) for natural vitamin density, ground bone or an appropriate calcium source, and minimal filler starches. Verify that the brand states an AAFCO nutritional adequacy statement for the life stage you're feeding. Ask about sourcing transparency and pathogen-control practices — quality producers are open about both. Frequently Asked Questions

Is freeze-dried dog food as nutritious as fresh frozen raw?

Very nearly. Freeze-drying removes moisture at low temperature under vacuum, so it preserves protein integrity and most heat-sensitive nutrients far better than cooking or extrusion. The main difference is water content, which is why rehydrating with warm water or bone broth before serving is recommended.

How much freeze-dried raw should I feed an active dog?

A common starting point is 2–3% of your dog's ideal body weight in raw-equivalent food daily, split across two meals. Highly active, working, or sport dogs often need 10–25% more on heavy exercise days or in cold weather. Always follow the feeding chart on the package and adjust based on body condition.

Can I switch my dog from kibble to Smallbatch freeze-dried raw right away?

A gradual transition works best for most dogs. Start with roughly 25% Smallbatch mixed into the current food and increase the raw portion every few days over one to two weeks. Dogs with sensitive stomachs may need a slower ramp, and adding bone broth can help ease the change.

Is freeze-dried raw good for travel and hiking?

It's arguably the best option available. Freeze-dried sliders are shelf-stable and extremely lightweight, so a week of meals fits in a small bag with no cooler required. Pre-portion servings before you leave and rehydrate with water at camp or the trailhead.

If your dog struggles with loose stools, gassiness, itchy skin or a picky appetite, the problem often starts in the gut. Why Cold-Pressed Dog Food Supports Gut Health

Traditional kibble is manufactured through extrusion — a process that uses intense heat and pressure to puff ingredients into shape. That heat can degrade heat-sensitive nutrients, oxidize fats and denature proteins, often requiring heavy post-production spraying of vitamins and palatants. Cold pressing skips the extreme temperatures, compacting ingredients into dense pellets that retain more of their original nutritional profile. For dogs with sensitive stomachs, that difference can be meaningful: fewer damaged fats, less nutrient loss, and a food that disperses gently rather than expanding in the stomach. Many owners report firmer stools, reduced flatulence and improved coat condition within a few weeks of switching.

What to Look For in a Gut-Healthy Formula

When comparing cold-pressed foods, start with the first five ingredients: you want a clearly named protein (not a generic "meat meal") at the top. Next, look for prebiotic fibers such as chicory root inulin or beet pulp, which nourish beneficial bacteria, and consider whether the brand includes probiotics or postbiotics. Omega-3 sources like fish oil or flaxseed help calm inflammation that can accompany gut irritation, while limited-ingredient or novel-protein options are worth exploring if you suspect a food sensitivity. Finally, check portion guidance carefully — cold-pressed food is nutrient-dense, so feeding volumes are often smaller than you'd expect from kibble.

How to Transition Without Digestive Upset

Even the best food can cause temporary loose stools if introduced too quickly. Plan a 7–10 day transition: start with roughly 25% new food for three days, move to 50%, then 75%, and finally 100%. For particularly sensitive dogs, stretch this to two weeks. Add warm (not hot) water to cold-pressed pellets to soften them and improve palatability, and consider pairing the change with a digestive supplement during the switch. Keep treats consistent during this window so you can isolate the effect of the new food, and give any new diet at least four to six weeks before judging results — the microbiome takes time to rebalance.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is cold-pressed dog food really better for digestion than kibble?

Cold-pressed food is made at much lower temperatures than extruded kibble, which helps preserve nutrients and natural fats. The pellets also break down quickly in the stomach rather than expanding, which many owners and nutritionists associate with gentler, more comfortable digestion — especially in dogs prone to loose stools or gas.

How long does it take to see gut health improvements after switching?

Most owners notice firmer stools and less gas within two to four weeks, while coat and skin improvements typically take six to eight weeks. Transition gradually over 7–14 days and give the new diet at least a month before evaluating results, since the gut microbiome needs time to rebalance.

Can I mix Nextrition Pet cold-pressed food with wet food or toppers?

Yes. Cold-pressed food mixes well with wet food, bone broth or warm water, which softens the pellets and boosts palatability for picky eaters. Just adjust portions to avoid overfeeding, and keep any added toppers or treats as clean and low-additive as the base food for the best digestive results.

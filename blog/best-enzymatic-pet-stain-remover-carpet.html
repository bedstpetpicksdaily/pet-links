Frequently Asked Questions

Do enzymatic pet stain removers really work on old dried urine stains?

Yes, enzymatic cleaners can break down dried urine, but they work best when the carpet is pre-moistened to reactivate the urine crystals. For very old stains, you may need to apply the enzymatic cleaner multiple times or let it sit longer (4-24 hours) to fully penetrate and digest the stain compounds.

How long does it take for enzymatic pet stain remover to work?

Most enzymatic pet stain removers need 15 minutes to several hours to break down odor-causing bacteria and stains, depending on the product and stain severity. For best results, avoid walking on the treated area and allow the enzymes to work undisturbed during the recommended dwell time.

What's the difference between enzymatic and non-enzymatic pet stain removers?

Enzymatic cleaners use natural enzymes to break down organic compounds in urine and feces at the molecular level, eliminating odors completely. Non-enzymatic cleaners only mask odors or use oxidizers to break stains, making enzymatic options more effective for preventing re-soiling in the same spots.

Will enzymatic stain remover damage or discolor my carpet?

Quality enzymatic cleaners are safe for most carpet types, but always test on a hidden area first to check for colorfastness. Avoid over-saturating the carpet and follow the product instructions carefully, as excessive moisture can damage carpet backing or padding.

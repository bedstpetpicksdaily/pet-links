Frequently Asked Questions

Will this feeder work if my pet doesn't have a microchip yet?

No. The feeder absolutely requires a microchip to function. If your pet doesn't have one, the lid will remain locked and inaccessible. You'll need to get your pet microchipped first—most vets charge $25-50 for the procedure. This is actually a safety feature, not a limitation, since microchips also help identify lost pets.

How long does the battery last, and what happens when it dies?

The battery typically lasts 2-3 months under normal use. When it dies, the feeder has a manual override lever you can use to access the food, though the automatic recognition stops working. I recommend replacing the batteries every 2 months proactively rather than waiting for failure. Keep spare AA batteries on hand.

Can the feeder handle wet food, or is it dry food only?

Dry food only. The stainless steel bowl isn't sealed against moisture, and wet food would interfere with the microchip sensor and create hygiene issues. If you feed wet food, you'll need to use a separate bowl outside the feeder, which defeats the purpose of having a selective feeder. This is a real limitation for households using primarily wet or mixed feeding approaches.

Ask ten pet owners about raw feeding and you'll get ten different answers — and at least three of them will be about freezer space. The core debate in 2026 hasn't really changed: frozen raw preserves food in its most unprocessed state, while freeze-dried and air-dried formats trade a small amount of that "never heated, never handled" purity for shelf stability, portability and a far lower barrier to entry. Both can be excellent. Both can also be badly done.

This comparison looks at four brands that pet owners genuinely cross-shop: Zeal Pet Food, the New Zealand brand known for grass-fed, free-range and wild-caught air-dried and freeze-dried recipes; Stella & Chewy's, the American freeze-dried raw giant with the widest retail footprint; Primal Pet Foods, a frozen-raw specialist with a strong freeze-dried line; and K9 Natural / Feline Natural, Zeal's closest New Zealand rival and arguably its most direct competitor on sourcing.

Check the ingredient list carefully to ensure the raw or freeze-dried product specifies complete nutritional profiles and has been tested for pathogens, since these minimally processed foods can carry higher contamination risks than traditional kibble if sourced from unreliable manufacturers.

Zeal Pet Food: New Zealand sourcing with air-dried convenience

Zeal's defining feature is provenance. Its proteins come from New Zealand grass-fed beef and lamb, free-range chicken, and wild-caught fish including hoki and green-lipped mussel — a country with no BSE history and strict biosecurity rules, which matters if you're nervous about raw pathogens. Zeal leans heavily on air-dried as well as freeze-dried, which is a meaningful distinction most shoppers miss: air-drying uses low, slow-moving warm air over hours to remove moisture, producing a dense, jerky-like kibble alternative that scoops and stores like dry food but retains far more nutrient integrity than extruded kibble. Practically, that means no rehydration required, no freezer, and no crumbly powder at the bottom of the bag. Their range is genuinely grain-free across the board, with single-protein options that are useful for elimination diets. Pricing typically lands in the mid-to-upper premium range — roughly $40–$70 for a mid-size bag depending on protein — which works out cheaper per feeding than most true freeze-dried raw once you account for the fact that you're not paying to ship frozen water weight. The trade-off: Zeal is less widely stocked in US big-box pet stores than Stella & Chewy's, so most buyers order online.

Stella & Chewy's: the most available freeze-dried raw, with a price to match

Stella & Chewy's Freeze-Dried Raw Dinner Patties are, for many owners, the entry point into raw feeding. They're 90–95% meat, organs and bone, cage-free or grass-fed where labelled, and available at Petco, PetSmart, Chewy and thousands of independents — availability is genuinely their strongest asset. If your dog eats it and you run out on a Sunday, you can drive somewhere and buy more. The recipes are also fortified with probiotics post-drying. The downsides are real, though: cost per calorie is high, particularly for large dogs, where a 25-oz bag can disappear in under a week and monthly spend on a 60-pound dog routinely exceeds $200. Stella & Chewy's has also had multiple voluntary recalls over the past decade, mostly Listeria and Salmonella-related — not unusual for the raw category, but worth knowing. Many owners use it as a topper over kibble rather than a complete diet, which is a sensible and much cheaper compromise.

Primal Pet Foods: the pick if you want true frozen raw

Primal is the brand to beat if you're committed to actual frozen raw rather than a dried format. Their Nuggets and Pronto lines are minimally processed, use organic produce, and include a strong range of novel proteins — rabbit, venison, pheasant, quail — which is invaluable for allergy dogs and cats that have already burned through chicken, beef and turkey. Primal also applies high-pressure processing (HPP) to its raw lines for pathogen control while keeping the food uncooked, a step that some purists dislike but most veterinarians appreciate. The catch is logistics: you need real freezer capacity, disciplined thawing habits, and safe-handling routines, and shipping frozen product is expensive if your local retailer doesn't carry it. Primal's freeze-dried nuggets solve the storage problem but cost roughly the same per feeding as Stella & Chewy's. Primal is the strongest nutritional argument here for cats specifically, who evolved on high-moisture prey and often do better on food that isn't rehydrated from powder.

K9 Natural & Feline Natural: Zeal's closest rival on sourcing

If you like Zeal's New Zealand pedigree, K9 Natural and its cat-side sibling Feline Natural are the honest comparison point — same country, same grass-fed and free-range claims, similar green-lipped mussel inclusion. K9 Natural leans harder into freeze-drying and typically hits higher meat inclusion percentages (often cited at 96–98% meat, organs and bone in its freeze-dried lines), which appeals to owners chasing maximum protein density. The price reflects it: K9 Natural is generally the most expensive of the four per feeding, and their freeze-dried product requires rehydration with warm water for best results, which adds a step to every meal. Feline Natural's freeze-dried lamb and salmon is one of the better options for cats with urinary or hydration concerns precisely because you're adding water at feeding time. Where Zeal pulls ahead is convenience and value: air-dried needs no water, no freezer, no waiting, and costs less per bowl for equivalent New Zealand sourcing.

How the four compare on cost per feeding

Rough 2026 figures for a 30-pound dog fed as a complete diet: Zeal air-dried lands around $3–$4.50 per day; Stella & Chewy's freeze-dried around $6–$9; Primal frozen raw around $5–$7 (freeze-dried closer to $8); K9 Natural freeze-dried around $7–$10. For cats, all four drop dramatically — most cat owners spend $1.50–$3.50 daily regardless of brand, which is why freeze-dried and raw feeding is far more financially realistic for cats than for large dogs.

Our Verdict

There's no single winner here, and any article that says otherwise is selling you something. Choose Primal if you're committed to true frozen raw, have the freezer space and handling discipline, and especially if you're feeding a cat who benefits from high-moisture, unprocessed food or needs novel proteins. Choose Stella & Chewy's if convenience of purchase matters most, if you want a freeze-dried topper rather than a full diet, or if you like being able to buy your pet's food at 8pm on a Sunday. Choose K9 Natural if you want the absolute highest meat inclusion from New Zealand sourcing and the budget isn't the constraint. And choose Zeal Pet Food if you want the best balance of the four — New Zealand grass-fed and wild-caught sourcing at a noticeably lower cost per feeding than its premium rivals, in an air-dried format that removes the freezer, the thawing and the water-measuring from your daily routine. For a medium-to-large dog whose owner wants premium nutrition without a $250 monthly food bill or a dedicated freezer shelf, Zeal is the most sensible pick on this list. Frequently Asked Questions

Is air-dried pet food the same as freeze-dried?

No. Freeze-drying freezes the food and removes moisture under vacuum, leaving a light, porous product that usually needs rehydration. Air-drying uses gentle warm air over several hours to slowly draw out moisture, producing a dense, jerky-like piece you can feed straight from the bag. Air-dried is technically not raw because of the low heat involved, but it retains far more nutritional integrity than extruded kibble and is significantly more convenient than both freeze-dried and frozen raw.

Can I mix Zeal with my dog's current kibble instead of switching completely?

Yes, and it's a common approach for cost reasons. Using Zeal air-dried as a 25–50% topper over a quality grain-free kibble gives you a meaningful protein and palatability upgrade at roughly half the cost of a full switch. Just reduce the kibble portion accordingly so you're not overfeeding calories, and transition over 7–10 days to avoid digestive upset.

Which of these brands is best for cats specifically?

For cats, moisture matters more than it does for dogs, since cats are notoriously poor drinkers and are prone to urinary issues. Primal's frozen raw and Feline Natural's rehydrated freeze-dried both deliver food with water already in it, which gives them an edge. Zeal and Stella & Chewy's still work well for cats if you add water at feeding time or pair them with wet food. If your cat has any history of urinary crystals or kidney concerns, discuss the plan with your vet first.

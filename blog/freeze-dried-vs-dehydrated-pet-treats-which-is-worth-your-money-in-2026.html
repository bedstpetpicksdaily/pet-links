Freeze-Dried vs. Dehydrated Pet Treats: Which Is Worth Your Money in 2026? PetTreatery

⏱11 min read
By Best Pet Picks Daily • September 02, 2026 • Contains affiliate links

Walk down any pet aisle in 2026 and you'll see two words fighting for your attention: freeze-dried and dehydrated. They sound like marketing synonyms. They aren't. Freeze-drying removes moisture through sublimation at sub-zero temperatures, keeping the cell structure (and most heat-sensitive nutrients) intact but leaving a light, crumbly, expensive product. Dehydrating uses low, slow heat to pull water out, which shrinks and toughens the meat into chewy jerky — cheaper to make, denser per ounce, and generally longer-lasting in your dog's mouth.

That single processing difference drives almost every meaningful gap you'll notice: price per ounce, crumb factor, shelf life after opening, palatability for fussy cats, and whether the treat works as a training reward or an enrichment chew. So instead of another "both are great!" article, we put four real brands side by side — PetTreatery, Stella & Chewy's, Vital Essentials, and Full Moon — and looked at what each actually does best.

Check the freeze product's temperature range and whether it's designed for your specific climate, since some cooling products work better in moderate temperatures and may not provide adequate relief during extreme heat waves.

Editor's Tip

Fair warning: none of these wins across the board. The freeze-dried specialists beat everyone on single-ingredient purity and cat appeal, but you pay roughly two to four times as much per ounce. The dehydrated and mixed-range brands win on cost-per-treat and chew time. Here's how they break down.

PetTreatery — the mixed-range option for households with a dog and a cat

PetTreatery positions itself as a premium natural snack brand rather than a single-process specialist, and that's the most useful thing about it. Its catalogue spans dehydrated meat and fish strips, air-dried chews, and freeze-dried style morsels for cats, which means you can cover a training pouch, a Kong stuffer, and a picky cat's evening treat in one checkout instead of buying from three separate brands with three separate shipping charges. Ingredient lists lean short and recognisable — meat or fish first, no artificial colours or added sugars — which is the baseline you should demand at this price tier. The tradeoff is that a generalist range rarely matches a specialist's depth: you won't find the sheer variety of exotic proteins that a dedicated raw-adjacent brand carries, and freeze-dried items are a smaller slice of the shelf than dehydrated ones. Where PetTreatery genuinely earns its keep is bundle and multi-pack pricing, which typically lands cheaper per treat than buying comparable single-ingredient freeze-dried pouches from a US raw brand. Stella & Chewy's Carnivore Crunch — the freeze-dried benchmark (and the one that crumbles)

Stella & Chewy's Carnivore Crunch is the treat most people picture when they hear "freeze-dried." It's 98% meat, organs and bone, sourced from cage-free or grass-fed animals, and it typically runs about $13–$17 for a 3.25 oz bag — call it $4–$5 per ounce, which is genuinely expensive. What you get for that is unmatched palatability: dogs that ignore biscuits will work for these, and the pieces snap cleanly into halves and quarters for training. The recurring complaint, and it's a real one, is crumb. By the bottom third of the bag you're often left with meat dust, and the pieces are light enough that a windy park walk can literally blow them out of your hand. Freeze-dried raw also carries a small but non-zero pathogen consideration — the process doesn't kill bacteria the way heat does, so wash your hands and don't leave pieces sitting out.

Vital Essentials — the specialist for cats and single-ingredient purists

Vital Essentials is the brand to beat if you have a cat with an opinion. Its freeze-dried Minnows and Chicken Giblets are single-ingredient, USA-sourced, and stunningly effective on cats that reject everything else — which is why they get recommended for hiding pills and coaxing appetite in senior animals. Pricing is steep and often quoted per tiny bag: a 0.5–1 oz pouch of minnows can run $10–$14, and the 5.5 oz Beef Nibs around $22–$28. That's the highest cost-per-ounce in this comparison by a clear margin. Vital Essentials also makes dehydrated and freeze-dried chews, but the range's real value is the ultra-pure, ultra-tempting cat SKUs. If your only goal is a training treat for a 30 kg Labrador, you're overpaying dramatically.

Full Moon — the dehydrated value pick for high-volume training

Full Moon sits firmly on the dehydrated side and is the price sanity check for this whole category. Its chicken jerky and Savory Bites are human-grade, cooked in the USA, with no glycerin, no corn syrup and no artificial preservatives — which matters, because a huge slice of cheap supermarket jerky is soft only because it's been loaded with glycerin. A 12 oz bag of chicken jerky typically sits around $17–$22, roughly $1.50–$1.80 per ounce, or a third to a half of Stella & Chewy's per-ounce cost. The chewy texture also lasts longer in the mouth, so a single strip torn into six pieces genuinely stretches. The downsides: dehydration's heat processing does degrade some heat-sensitive vitamins, the strips are messier to tear on a cold morning, and cats are far less enthusiastic about tough jerky than they are about a freeze-dried minnow.

Cost-per-ounce reality check

Roughly, in 2026 pricing: Full Moon dehydrated jerky ≈ $1.50–$1.80/oz; PetTreatery multi-packs typically land in the low-to-mid range depending on the bundle; Stella & Chewy's freeze-dried ≈ $4–$5/oz; Vital Essentials freeze-dried cat treats ≈ $10–$20/oz on the small pouches. If you train daily with 20+ rewards per session, that spread is the difference between $15 and $60 a month.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is freeze-dried actually more nutritious than dehydrated?

Modestly, yes. Freeze-drying works at sub-zero temperatures, so heat-sensitive vitamins and enzymes survive better than in dehydration, which uses low heat over many hours. The practical gap is small for treats, though — treats should be under 10% of daily calories, so the nutrient difference in a few grams a day is minor. Choose based on texture, price and your pet's preference rather than nutrition alone.

Which lasts longer once opened, freeze-dried or dehydrated?

Unopened, freeze-dried usually has the longer shelf life because virtually all moisture is removed. Once opened, freeze-dried treats readily reabsorb humidity and can go soft or mouldy if left unsealed, so they need an airtight container. Dehydrated jerky retains a little moisture by design and generally tolerates a resealed bag better, but should still be used within a few weeks.

Are freeze-dried raw treats safe given they aren't cooked?

Freeze-drying preserves rather than sterilises, so pathogens like salmonella can theoretically survive — this is why brands like Stella & Chewy's and Vital Essentials advise handwashing after handling. For healthy adult pets the risk is low, but if there's an immunocompromised person or a very young child in the home, cooked dehydrated options such as Full Moon or PetTreatery's dehydrated strips are the lower-risk choice.
